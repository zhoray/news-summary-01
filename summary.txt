World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026:
Russia, Ukraine and the US ended two days of talks in Abu Dhabi, their first in nine months, with a big prisoner swap reported agreed in principle; Russia rejected a ceasefire and territory unresolved (Reuters).
In the Strait of Hormuz a tanker was struck off Oman and Iran's Guards said others hit mines on unauthorised routes (unconfirmed), while oil rose over the waterway carrying a fifth of world oil (Reuters).
World leaders opened a summit at the COP31 climate talks in Antalya, hosted by Turkey's President Erdogan, pushing a "35 by 35" electrification pledge; the US stayed away a second year (AFP).
The US House voted 211-204 to send impeachment charges against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to a committee, blocking the bid (BBC).
The US charged three men over alleged Iran-backed plots, one against RAF Fairford in Britain (BBC), and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base over Iranian threats (New York Times).
A Florida judge let President Trump's $10bn defamation suit against the BBC proceed, over a misleadingly edited clip of his 6 January speech (Reuters).
Mississippi's Senate race remained uncalled, Democrat Scott Colom leading Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,600 votes, 97% counted; Democrats already hold a 54-seat majority (CBS).
Spain's first general strike in 14 years, backed by the UGT and CCOO unions, targeted a housing shortage put at about 750,000 homes (Reuters).
Asian shares fell after US October inflation beat forecasts (prices up 0.4% on the month, core up 0.3%), with markets fully pricing a December Fed rate rise (Reuters).
Congo's Ebola cases passed 11,042 confirmed, with 5,387 deaths: the second-largest outbreak on record, of a strain with no approved vaccine. Kenya released its last patient (Reuters).
President Bukele said El Salvador's troops destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters in Haiti; a reported toll of about 20 dead was unconfirmed (BBC).
