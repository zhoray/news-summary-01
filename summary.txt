Wednesday 11 November 2026 — world news
Russia, Ukraine and the United States held two days of Abu Dhabi talks, their first in nine months. A large prisoner swap was reported agreed in principle, but Russia rejected a ceasefire and territory remained unresolved. (Reuters).
A tanker was struck off Oman in the Strait of Hormuz, which carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said ships hit mines after sailing unauthorised routes, an unconfirmed claim; oil prices rose. (Reuters).
World leaders opened COP31's summit in Antalya, Turkey, hosted by President Erdogan, promoting the “35 by 35” electrification pledge. The US stayed away for a second year. (AFP).
The US House voted 211–204 to send impeachment charges against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking the bid. (BBC).
The US charged three men over alleged Iran-backed plots, including one targeting RAF Fairford. (BBC). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base over Iranian threats. (New York Times).
A Florida judge let President Trump's $10bn defamation suit against the BBC proceed over a misleadingly edited clip of his 6 January speech. (Reuters).
Mississippi's Senate race remained uncalled: Democrat Scott Colom led Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,600 votes, with 97% counted. Democrats already held a 54-seat majority either way. (CBS).
Spain's first general strike in 14 years, backed by UGT and CCOO, targeted a 750,000-home housing shortage. (Reuters).
Asian shares fell after US October inflation beat forecasts: consumer prices rose 0.4%, core prices 0.3%. Markets fully priced in a December Fed rate rise. (Reuters).
Congo's confirmed Ebola cases passed 11,042, with 5,387 deaths, the second-largest outbreak on record. The strain has no approved vaccine; Kenya discharged its last patient. (Reuters).
President Bukele said El Salvador's soldiers destroyed Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang headquarters. A reported death toll of about 20 was unconfirmed. (BBC).
